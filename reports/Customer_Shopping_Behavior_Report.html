Retail Customer Behavior Analytics

Project Report — Data Preparation, SQL Analysis & Power BI Dashboard

Python PostgreSQL Power BI

1. Project Overview

This project examines shopping behavior across 3,900 retail transactions to understand what drives customer spending, product preference, and subscription loyalty. The goal is to turn raw transactional data into a set of actionable insights that a retail management team could use to shape marketing, discounting, and product strategy.

Key fields fall into three groups:

  • Purchase details — item purchased, category, purchase amount, season, size, color
  • Shopping behavior — discount applied, promo code used, previous purchases, purchase frequency, review rating, shipping type

3. Data Preparation (Python)

The raw dataset was cleaned and transformed in pandas before being loaded into PostgreSQL:

  • Missing data handling — the 37 missing values in review_rating were imputed with each product category's median rating, rather than a single global median, to keep the imputation category-aware.
  • Column standardization — all column names were converted to snake_case for consistency between Python, SQL, and Power BI.
  • Feature engineering — an age_group column (Young Adult / Adult / Middle-aged / Senior) was created via quartile binning, and a purchase_frequency_days column translated categorical purchase frequency into a numeric day count.
  • Redundancy check — discount_applied and promo_code_used were found to be identical across every row, so promo_code_used was dropped.
  • Database load — the cleaned DataFrame was loaded into PostgreSQL via SQLAlchemy for structured SQL analysis.

5. Dashboard in Power BI

The cleaned data was connected to Power BI (Import mode via PostgreSQL) to build an interactive dashboard, with filters for subscription status, gender, age group, and shipping type.

6. Business Recommendations

  • Boost subscriptions — since subscribers and non-subscribers spend at similar averages, promote exclusive subscriber benefits to convert high-frequency non-subscribers rather than relying on discounting alone.
  • Invest in customer loyalty programs — reward repeat buyers to move more of the "Returning" segment into "Loyal," and study what differentiates the small "New" segment to improve acquisition.
  • Review discount policy — products like Hats and Sneakers rely on discounts for roughly half of their sales; assess whether margin is being eroded unnecessarily on items that would sell at full price.
  • Lead with top-rated products in campaigns — Gloves, Sandals, and Boots have the highest average ratings and are natural candidates for featured placement or upsell bundles.
  • Target high-revenue segments — Young Adults and Express-shipping users show slightly higher spend; tailor campaigns and shipping incentives toward these groups first.
